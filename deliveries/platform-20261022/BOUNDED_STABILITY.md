# Bounded live observation and operation-time status records

Completed on 2026-10-24. Approximately 904 seconds of live observation produced 61 samples at a 15-second target interval. Every sample verified both services, candidate identity, source/record counts, readiness, the retrieval service's disabled public-model path and unchanged approved quota/expiry. No sample failed; the largest observed inter-sample gap was about 15.013 seconds. Combined status-read latency had P50 281 ms, P95 312 ms and maximum 328 ms. These are status-request timings, not general question-answer latency claims.

Three rounds of four historical positive/negative list/table questions produced twelve local source-only checks. Two existing ordinary hybrid-retrieval questions were each checked twice, adding four checks. Answer and source fingerprints remained stable. This is sixteen calls across six historical question variants, not sixteen independent new business topics or new model-generation acceptance. Source-only requests append technical records to the shared preview history; the daily workbench registry was unchanged.

Both service processes retained their PID and creation time. Resource measurements were retained without claiming proof of no memory leak or a load/stress test. Live registry, policy and prepared-runtime files remained byte-identical, with no new public-model call, quota reset, publication, service restart, backup creation/copy or restore.

The existing Windows management command now saves a unique status JSON at its service-check stage for success or not-ready results. It stores only time, readiness, candidate identity, source/record counts, recent-question display count and bounded quota/expiry metadata. It does not store question text, raw HTTP failures or credentials. A recording I/O failure warns without replacing the original readiness outcome. The command also labels its last-fifty-question display explicitly rather than presenting that display as the total history count.

Two new isolated Windows tests exercised successful and failing checks twice, preserving earlier records and readiness outcomes, excluding private payload sentinels and creating no backup. Final exported backend: 675 passed with three existing dependency warnings. The source manifest now contains 276 files. No frontend source or asset changed, so no frontend rebuild or service restart was needed. Two actual same-day operation-time records were also verified.

These records are operation-triggered checkpoints, not a scheduler, continuous monitor or alerting service. Gaps have unknown availability, and two records on one day do not prove multi-day stability. Existing process ages are not substituted for the observed window. The private handoff consolidates the three still-pending business answers and preserves the owner's decision to defer backup copying. Neither business acceptance nor multi-day/full-recovery acceptance is inferred from this phase.
